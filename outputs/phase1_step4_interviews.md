## Who to talk to

Local freelance web developers and small agencies (1-3 people) who build websites for small businesses. Find them through local business associations, online directories like Upwork or Fiverr, and by reaching out to clients of existing web development firms.

## Questions (about past behaviour, never pitching)

1. What is your typical process for starting a website project with a client?
2. How do you handle changes in scope requested by the client during development?
3. Have you ever had to redo work due to scope creep, and if so, what was the impact on your time and money?
4. What are the common reasons clients ask for additional features or changes after the initial contract is signed?
5. How do you track project progress and ensure that both you and the client are aligned on the scope of work?
6. Have you ever used any tools or methods to manage expectations with clients before development starts?
7. What happens when a client requests a change that was not originally outlined in the contract?
8. What steps do you take to prevent misunderstandings about project scope at the beginning of a project?

## Signals to listen for

- Descriptions of past scope creep incidents
- Mentions of tools or processes used to manage expectations
- Comments on time and money spent on changes
- Experiences with client communication during development
- Methods used to align client and developer expectations

## Commitments to ask for

- A follow-up call to discuss specific instances of scope creep
- Trial use of a prototype tool for managing project scope
- Sharing a summary of past experiences with clients regarding project expectations
